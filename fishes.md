# Fishes

Fishes are aquatic vertebrate animals that live in oceans, seas, rivers, lakes, and other water bodies around the world. They are among the most diverse groups of animals, with over 34,000 known species ranging from tiny gobies to massive whale sharks.

Most fishes breathe through gills, which extract oxygen from water, and use fins for swimming, balance, and steering. Their bodies are often covered with scales that help protect them from predators and environmental hazards.

Fishes play a vital role in aquatic ecosystems by maintaining food chains and ecological balance. They serve as both predators and prey, helping regulate populations of other organisms. In addition, fishes are an important source of food, income, and recreation for millions of people worldwide.

Fishes can be broadly classified into three main groups:

* **Jawless Fishes** – Primitive fishes without jaws, such as lampreys.
* **Cartilaginous Fishes** – Fishes with skeletons made of cartilage, such as sharks and rays.
* **Bony Fishes** – The largest group, with skeletons made primarily of bone, including salmon, tuna, and goldfish.

Their remarkable adaptability allows them to thrive in environments ranging from shallow freshwater streams to the deepest parts of the ocean.
